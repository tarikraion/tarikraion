Who needs GPUs live between 30 Jan and 1 Apr 2027

Raion's best buyers for this window are funded AI companies that train or serve their own models and need dedicated capacity of roughly 200 to 4,000 GPUs (one to twenty 0.5 MW blocks) on a 12 to 36 month term. Behind them come regulated enterprises and sovereign buyers in the UK and EU, who need capacity they control in their own jurisdiction. Because dedicated clusters take months to contract and hand over, every buyer who needs capacity in February or March 2027 is choosing a supplier between October and December 2026. They do that at C-level.

Timing

The 120–180 day window, and when the decision is made

Today plus 120 days is 30 January 2027; plus 180 days is 1 April 2027. A buyer who needs a cluster running in that window has to sign this quarter. Raion's first capacity goes live from November 2026, ahead of the window.

Segments

Four buyer segments, in priority order

Segments 1 and 2 are the volume: fast decisions, founder-led, triggered by funding. Segments 3 and 4 are fewer, larger and slower, but they fit Raion's ownership and sovereignty model best.

1 · Scaling model builders

Priority
Who
Series A–D AI companies that pre-train, post-train or fine-tune their own models: generative video, image, audio and voice; robotics, world models and physical AI; AI for science and drug discovery; open-weight and code models.
Size
20–1,000 staff; $50M+ raised, latest round in the last 6 months.
Need
One to eight blocks (about 200–2,000 Blackwell GPUs), dedicated, InfiniBand, 12–24 months, for a training run that starts in Q1 2027.
Why now
New money is earmarked for compute, and the next model is already on the roadmap. Hyperscaler credits run out about when the run starts.
Who signs
CEO (founder) with the CTO or Chief Scientist; the CFO signs from Series C up.
Lead with
A cluster that is yours alone, live on the date your run starts, with no thermal throttling.

2 · Inference-heavy AI products

Priority
Who
AI-native products growing fast that serve their own or open-weight models: agents, voice, avatars, search, copilots and vertical AI (legal, health, finance).
Size
$10M+ ARR or fast usage growth; $50M+ raised.
Need
One to four blocks (about 200–1,000 GPUs) of steady inference, often in the EU or UK for data residency, at a predictable unit cost.
Why now
Usage is outgrowing on-demand capacity, and inference margins are under board pressure. EU customers ask where data is processed.
Who signs
CEO and CTO; the CFO owns the gross-margin case.
Lead with
A fixed price per GPU-hour for the term and in-region capacity that lifts gross margin.

3 · Sovereign and regulated enterprises

Strategic
Who
Banks, insurers, telcos, pharma, defence, energy, industrials and public bodies in the UK and EU, plus quant and trading firms in the US, UK and EU, that build AI in-house.
Size
1,000+ staff or a regulated entity; an approved AI budget or a named AI programme.
Need
0.5–10 MW they own or control, in jurisdiction, isolated from other tenants.
Why now
2026 AI programmes are moving from pilots to production; data-sovereignty rules and board risk reviews are pushing workloads off shared US clouds.
Who signs
CIO, CTO or Chief AI Officer with the CFO; the CEO for strategic or sovereign deals.
Lead with
Capacity you own through an SPV under British common law, specified across several vendors.

4 · Capital and power owners

Financing side
Who
Family offices, energy and utility companies, colocation and real-estate owners with spare grid capacity, who want to own AI infrastructure as an asset.
Size
Spare power of 1 MW or more, or capital to commit in 0.5 MW increments.
Need
Standardised modular GPU shells that turn power into revenue, with an operator and offtake attached.
Why now
Grid queues and hyperscale backlash make distributed modular capacity the fastest route to a yielding asset.
Who signs
CEO, CIO (investments) and CFO.
Lead with
A yielding AI asset in 0.5 MW blocks, with demand from Raion's founder network.
Qualify

In or out in two minutes

Must have
  • Headquarters or main compute footprint in the US, UK or Europe.
  • Trains, fine-tunes or serves its own or open-weight models. Calling OpenAI or Anthropic APIs alone does not count.
  • Can use at least one 0.5 MW block (about 200–250 Blackwell GPUs) for 12 months or more. The sweet spot is 0.5–5 MW.
  • Can commit about $7M+ a year per block. That usually means $50M+ raised in a startup, or an approved AI budget in an enterprise.
  • A dated trigger in the last 6 months that points to more compute in Q1 2027.
  • A reachable C-level owner: CEO, CTO, Chief Scientist, Chief AI Officer, CIO, CFO or COO.
Disqualify
  • Frontier labs and hyperscalers (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Meta, Google, Microsoft, Amazon). They buy direct at gigawatt scale.
  • GPU clouds and neoclouds. They are competitors; treat them as channel partners at most.
  • API-wrapper products with no model of their own.
  • Needs less than a block, or only short bursts of on-demand hours. Send seed and Series A teams to a pooled or forum offer instead.
  • Pre-seed or unfunded teams that cannot commit to a 12-month term.
  • Infrastructure already locked in a multi-year deal that runs past mid-2027.
